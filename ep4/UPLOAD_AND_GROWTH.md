## Why Episodes 1–3 got few views (honest diagnosis)
I can't see your YouTube Studio, so these are the most likely causes, not certainties:

1. **Nobody searches story titles.** Parents search "counting 1 to 10", "learn colors" or "nursery rhymes", not "Milo and the Windy Day". A brand-new channel gets almost no browse or recommendation traffic, so search and Shorts are the only doors in. Episode 4 is built around a searched topic for that reason.
2. **Made for Kids limits discovery tools.** On these videos YouTube turns off comments and personalized ads, and it can remove features such as end screens and the ability to save to playlists ([YouTube best practices](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10774223), [vidIQ](https://www.vidiq.com/blog/post/make-money-kids-youtube-channel)). That's why the blank end-screen space is cut from Episode 4.
3. **YouTube rewards high-quality kids content and holds back low-quality content.** Its official guidance says high-quality made-for-kids content is raised up in recommendations ([YouTube best practices](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10774223)). Our cutout animation (no lip-sync, stiff poses) is the biggest weakness compared with channels that use full animation. Episode 4 leans on bright colors, constant motion, counting and repetition, which suits this style better than story scenes. It is still not studio-quality animation.
4. **Three videos is very early.** Channels that grow post on a steady schedule. Short-form clips act as a funnel that sends viewers to the longer videos ([AIR interview with a 23M kids channel](https://air.io/en/inside-jason-vlogs-interview-with-the-creator-of-a-23m-kids-channel)). Consistency matters more than one viral hit for Made for Kids content ([Whizzy Studios](https://www.whizzystudios.com/post/the-ultimate-guide-to-3d-kids-content-for-youtube-creators-in-2026)).
5. **Songs and learning videos dominate kids' YouTube** ([Kidscreen](https://kidscreen.com/?p=144378)), and preschoolers respond to repetition and expressive characters ([Whizzy Studios](https://www.whizzystudios.com/post/the-ultimate-guide-to-3d-kids-content-for-youtube-creators-in-2026)). Stories can still work later, once people know the channel.

**I can't promise 500–1000 views.** Nobody honestly can; it depends on YouTube's systems and on your channel's history. What I can do is make each upload as findable and watchable as possible, then adjust based on your numbers.

## How to run
```bash
pip install -r build/requirements.txt          # plus ffmpeg
# put your images in pack/ (same filenames as the original pack) and the clips in clips/shot01.mp4 ... shot06.mp4
KOKORO_DIR=/path/to/kokoro ./build/run.sh      # audio -> timeline -> final check
./build/run.sh --subtitles                     # same, with burned-in subtitles
python3 build/qc.py clip clips/shot01.mp4 --keyframe pack/SHOT_01_CAR_CHASE.png --need 5   # check one clip on its own
```
- `build/audio.py --measure` shows whether each line fits its shot.
- The timeline stops with a list of problems if a clip is missing, too short, or fails the checks. With `--allow-flagged` it builds only a `..._FLAGGED_NOT_FINAL.mp4`.
- Every build writes `final/build_log.json` (clip hashes, native resolution of each clip, warnings, ffmpeg version) so the result is reproducible.

## What the checker can and cannot do
It tests: native resolution, frame rate, duration, frozen frames, **real motion vs a camera move on a still** (separates camera zoom/pan from independent motion), static corner overlays (watermarks), whether the clip starts from its keyframe, abrupt cuts and morphs, and for the final: size, codecs, loudness, unexpected black frames.
It cannot confirm that a face matches the sheet, that hands are right, that the car is the Shot 1 car, that wheels turn correctly or that text is unreadable. Those are on the contact sheet it writes, and need a person's eyes (I do that review when you send clips). On very static scenes it also cannot find watermarks, which is why it saves the four corner crops.
How it was tested: planted flaws in stand-in clips made from your keyframes. A pure zoom, a smooth zoom and a pan were all rejected; a clip with a moving object passed; a drawn watermark, a wrong starting picture and a 720p clip were flagged.

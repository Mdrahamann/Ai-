## What has to be made

- **63 AI motion clips** covering **464 s** of the 480 s film, plus 2 title cards (16 s) that need no clip.
- Two of those clips exist as **prototype footage** (1.01 and 7.03). They show the wrong cars (a different supercar in each, one with a visible brand badge and a legible license plate), so they are placeholders to be **replaced**, not final.

## Cost, in credits (not money)

Planning assumption: an average of 1.5 generations per low-risk clip, 2.0 per medium-risk clip and 3.0 per high-risk clip (car motion, running), because cars, hands and faces fail often. That is about **136 generations and 1000 s of generated video** to get 464 s of usable footage.

## Order of work (each step is a gate; stop and review at every gate)

1. **Gate 0, done:** prototype rough cut of 33.5 s (14 s AI clips, 19.5 s labelled stills) with voices and sound.
2. **Gate 1, car and images:** you approve the car turnaround (Step 0 in the kit) and the corrected Shots 3, 5, 6. Nothing else should be generated before the car is locked, because the car appears in 21 shots.
3. **Gate 2, the one real test:** one 5 s Shot 1 clip from the original Shot 1 image. If a free tool cannot keep the car's shape and wheels believable, **we stop and rethink the tool**, not the plan.
4. **Gate 3, the 32 s pilot:** six clips (Shots 1 to 6 of the pilot) with the voices already made. This is also the proof for you: does it look like the AAA game-cinema target?
5. **Scene by scene, hardest first within each scene:** car shots (H) before talking shots (M) before inserts (L), so a failing car shot is found early. Generate, run `qc.py`, look at the contact sheet, keep or retake. Do not generate the next scene until the current one passes.
6. **Final assembly** with `timeline.py` (real clips only), the quality check on the finished file, and the licence and disclosure checks.

## Paths forward (you choose; none is started)

- **A. Pay for credits later, when you decide to.** Cheapest sensible route: Higgsfield Kling standard, roughly 1,500 credits for the whole episode by the estimate above. I'd confirm the real plan price with you first and run nothing without your yes.
- **B. Build it slowly on free allowances** from several tools, a few clips at a time. Every free clip is 720p and watermarked, so it only works as a **preview version**, never a clean release, and the allowances change often.
- **C. A shorter first episode.** Keep the 8-minute screenplay as the master, but release a 2 to 3 minute **cold open + dawn scene** first (scenes 1 and 8 plus the title and tag: about 26 clips). Smaller, finishable, and a real test of whether the channel gets an audience before the big spend.
- **D. Hybrid with storyboard stills.** Real motion only for the 15 high-risk action clips and the hero shots; the rest as clearly labelled stills with the voices. It would not be a fully animated film, and I would label it that way.

## Quality rules for every clip (unchanged)

Real motion only (the checker rejects a still with a camera move); the car must be the Shot 1 car; Alex and Mia match their sheets; Alex is unarmed; no readable text, brand marks or license plates; no watermark; native 1080p preferred and any upscale recorded. The checker handles the numbers; the faces, hands, car shape and wheels are checked by eye on the contact sheet.
